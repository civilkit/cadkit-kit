# Research: SolidCraft / SolidCore blend code, and what it means for our fillets

Read-only study. Nothing was built or run from either repository, and no instruction found in their files was followed (all of it treated as data). Paths are relative to the clones: `<cadkit>/ext/solidcraft` (HEAD `58c64ca4`) and `<cadkit>/ext/solidcore` (shallow clone, HEAD `d688288`, 2026-11-01). Our kernel paths are relative to the kernel repo, read from local `main`. "Not verified" marks anything I did not confirm by reading code or running it. Companion to `docs/research-solidcraft.md`.

## 0. Summary

1. The thing worth looking at is not SolidCraft's own blend code. It is a small truck-based fillet (about 14.5k lines, mostly planar/straight-edge cases plus a sampled-and-fitted rolling ball) that SolidCraft itself is replacing (`ROADMAP.md` "SMLib kernel migration", `CONTRIBUTING.md` ~l.416).
2. **SolidCore is a Rust port of NVIDIA's SMLib** (USD BRep v1.0.1, Apache-2.0), not an independent kernel (`solidcore/NOTICE`, `ATTRIBUTION.md`). About 687k lines of Rust in `crates/solidcore/src`, of which the blend module is about 124k.
3. The real fillet engine is therefore SMLib's: a general rolling-ball trace (spine from offset-surface intersection, rails, section), with a corner (vertex blend) layer, setback, overflow and "no room" handling. SolidCraft's fork of SMLib (`tangency-labs/smlib`, public, Apache-2.0) adds a "generic blend" rework (`solidcore/docs/generic-blend.md`) that is only partly ported to Rust and mostly opt-in.
4. Licences are all permissive: SolidCraft MIT OR Apache-2.0; SolidCore and SMLib Apache-2.0 only. Reuse or derivation is allowed with notices (section 1). No third-party papers' code is embedded; the papers are cited in docs only.
5. Their B-rep model (arena topology, typed ids, coedges with pcurves, analytic and NURBS surfaces) is close to ours in shape, but the blend code is welded to the whole solidcore kernel (NURBS, surface classes, intersector, healing, kernel state). A cherry-pick port into `ck-ops/src/blend` is not realistic. 123k lines of blend need about 560k lines of the rest.
6. Design difference that matters: SMLib edits topology in place (trim faces on the rails, insert the blend face, build corner patches). Ours builds a tool solid and booleans it (`blend/freeform.rs`, `build.rs`). Corner and overflow behaviour follow from that choice.
7. Against our four failure classes: overflow is the best match (SMLib has a dedicated cliff-overflow mode that passes its cases); vertex blends are partly covered (planar-ish corners yes, curved-face corners and some concave junctions are on their known-failing list); curved neighbour ends partly; narrow wedge is refused with a size, not solved.
8. Their own corpus: 271 fillet and chamfer cases, 220 pass, 17 refused as expected, 34 known failing (counted on the C++ fork at `5e8c9c1`, not on the Rust port). Some of those 34 are the same failure classes as ours.
9. Recommendation: do not port code. Reimplement selected algorithms in our own code (with attribution where derived), starting with a measured experiment on the CI box (section 4), then stage the work (section 5).

## 2. What their blend code does

### 2.1 SolidCraft (truck), `crates/kernel/src`

| File | Lines | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `blend.rs` | 3121 | Straight edge between two planar faces, each end vertex touching exactly one more planar face; round, G2 quintic, chamfer. Header says edges at an already blended corner, curved edges and other configurations "are reported as not supported yet". |
| `blend_corner.rs` | 1583 | Radius per edge; straight convex edges, planar faces, three edges per vertex. One filleted edge ends on the third face; two meet in a crease (cylinders trim each other); three get a sphere (equal radii) or a rational patch (unequal). |
| `blend_vertex.rs` | 404 | Three equal-radius edges at a convex vertex of any smooth faces: the ball touching all three faces, sphere patch. Built by cutting the corner with three planes, rounding, then swapping the quarter discs for the sphere patch. |
| `curveblend.rs`, `curvecorner.rs` | 2257, 1246 | Rolling ball along a closed chain of smooth edges between two smooth face groups. The centre is solved at 24 samples per edge (cap 369), the blend face is a rational B-spline fitted through exact arcs. Corners: same 1-2-3 edge rules as above, on curved faces. Knife edges (opposite normals) handled via the `KNIFE` constant. |
| `corner_g2.rs` | 501 | G2 corner tensor patch. Refuses a folding patch or a corner with no room. |
| `blend_variable.rs`, `blend_fit.rs` | 880, 652 | Variable radius and chord width (planar straight edges); fit check against "wedges" (largest size that fits). |

Method: rolling ball with sampled and fitted surfaces (no certificate), tool-style trimming. Surface types in the curved path: any smooth face pair through a generic solve (planes, cylinders, cones, spheres, tori, splines in principle); the analytic fast paths are planar only. Validation: volume checks against analytic wedges (`blend_fit.rs`), SolidCraft's ~5,000 tests and a FreeCAD-computed oracle recipe set (`tests/oracle`, two of them corner blends: `36-corner-blend-two-radii`, `37-pyramid-apex-blend`). Documented limits (`ROADMAP.md` ~l.475): G2 fillets only between planar faces; "curved faces/edges, multi-face ends and corner junctions remain unsupported" for G2; a round after a round of the edge between the wall and a flat face that it would run over is excluded.

Verdict on SolidCraft's own code: a similar "good enough for common parts" scope to ours, with the same failure classes. Not a source of new capability.

### 2.2 SolidCore (SMLib port), `crates/solidcore/src/blend`

Algorithm (`docs/generic-blend.md` sections 2-3): rolling ball. Spine `m = O1 ∩ O2` of the two faces offset by `r` toward the blend; rails are the ball feet `m - r n_i` (their parameters on the offsets are the feet's parameters on the faces, so no extra solve); the cross-section is the chord (chamfer), the exact rational circle (round, order 1), or a Hartmann G^n section (order 2 to 4, with an equal-area thumbweight). Variable radius is the envelope of the balls, not the great-circle sweep. Constant, variable-law, chamfer and G2 all share one trace. "Events" end the trace: End (end face), tangent end, corner, overflow, fold or no-room refusal.

Exact classes (section 4): plane/plane (cylinder), plane/cylinder and coaxial pairs (torus), plane/cone (rational (4,2)), two cones or cone/cylinder and cone/sphere (higher degree rational), per Dahl and Krasauskas. Anything else uses the certified traced spine, with the net certified per span by interval/Krawczyk tests (`blend/fillet_section/certificate.rs`, `interval.rs`). Stored form: `SmProceduralBlend`, a definition plus a certified net.

Source layout (Rust): `fillet_executive` (session, API: `fillet_edges`, `fillet_edges_per_edge`, `variable_radius_fillet`), `fillet_solver` (trace), `fillet_geom`, `fillet_standard_solver`, `fillet_custom_solver`, `fillet_intersector`, `fillet_corner` (+ `topology/` with `closed_nx1`, `nx1_end`, `mixed_3x2`, `concave_4x2`, `concave_nxn`, `end_corners`), `blend_setback.rs`, `fillet_overflow.rs`, `fillet_cliff_overflow.rs`, `fillet_removal`, `fillet_section`, `fillet_exact`, `identify_fillet`, `hcr`.

Vertex blends (the first of our failures): the corner layer classifies each blended vertex once. Kinds: End (one blend ends on the end face), Join (G1 continuation), Turn (two blends sharing a face, turning at a sharp side edge: Mitre default, Swept torus optional), Ball (three equal rounds of one convexity: one rational (4,2) sphere patch), Setback (everything else: unequal radii, 4 or more edges, mixed convexity, G2). Setback fills a 2n-sided hole with one setback patch per blend plus an n-sided interior (setback split, Varady and Rockwood), least thin-plate energy, certified convex; default setback `max(1.5 r, contact minimum + 0.25 r)`, grown up to 2x until it certifies. Important status caveat from `blend/blend_setback.rs` itself: "Faces are planes (a curved face is not filled here yet)" and "Nothing in the kernel calls `distances`, `fill_hole` or `containment`" (they exist and are tested, but are not wired in). The upstream (pre-rework) corner code with its 3x2, 4x2, nxn classes is what actually runs.

Chains with corners: a chain is a run of tangent-continuous edges; a corner in the selection becomes a vertex with a Turn/End event (mitre by default). A "partly selected tangent chain" ends with a real End at the smooth vertex (their issue 81, open). Curved end faces: End events build the end curve `S ∩ E`; tangent ends (end face tangent to the rail's face along a seam) are built outward from the tangent point with a `s^(k/2)` law (fork step 1, merged in the fork, ported to solidcore as opt-in work, issue 2243). Narrow wedges: a fold and a "room" check (Lukacs conditions, `h_i(r) > 0`, no local self-intersection) refuse with the largest size that fits (`smlib_blend_fit_size`); they never trim or shrink silently. Overflow: the blend is kept and trimmed at its crease with the continued neighbour ("cliff" mode, `fillet_cliff_overflow.rs`, `fillet_overflow.rs`), or `fit reduce`/`taper` options.

Validation and tests: differential testing against the C++ original (`crates/difftest`, 554,873 cases on `main` CI of 2026-10-23: 550,446 pass, 0 fail, 4,186 deferred to unported calls, 7 known mismatches; 199 of 261 units green, 61 partial, none failing: `plan/STATUS.md`). End-to-end fillet/chamfer regression tests in Rust: `crates/solidcore/tests/` has 335 files, about 35 of them fillet/chamfer/blend named (`issue_418_fillet_edge_on_tangent_seam.rs`, `wr_542_fillet_overlapping_bands.rs`, `fillet_four_round_corner_sphere.rs`, `issue_1474_nx1_corner_tangent_round_end.rs`, ...). The SolidCraft rig gallery (`docs/generic-blend.md` section 7): 271 cases, 220 pass, 17 refused as expected, 34 known failing, counted on the C++ fork build.

Documented limits (the known-failing list, section 7, plus open issues 80-98):
- Mixed-convexity corners: G2 setback only where edges are all convex or all concave; a convex and a concave chamfer meeting where a face runs tangent into a cylinder or cone fails ("3x2 mixed-convex", status 1001).
- Three or more concave rounds at a vertex (a Y-junction of pipes): refused by the room check.
- Four and five edge apex corners with G2: default setback too short, refused.
- A cylinder tilted 20 or 40 degrees into a plate, root edge rounded: status 1001, also before the rework.
- Curved face at a ball or setback corner (their issue 85), G2 corner patch only G1 at one seam in several cases, equal-pipe tee crossing corner.
- Convex round wider than both faces, and a round 0.01 past a face: status 1001.
- Rounds that read back from STEP sometimes fail where the native build works (`report-peg-step-front-and-concave-rounds-together-r2`).

## 3. Data model and port surface

Their model: arena topology with typed ids, coedge ("edgeuse") + loop + face + shell, analytic and NURBS curves and surfaces, pcurves, a global `Kernel` value instead of C++ global context, error values instead of status codes (`NOTICE`: "arena topology, typed ids, error values..."). No `unsafe`, no deps beyond `thiserror`, designed to build for `wasm32-unknown-unknown` (README; stack size note: needs about 8 MiB on wasm). Ours (`ck-topo/src/body.rs`): `Body { lumps -> shells -> faces -> loops of coedges -> edges -> vertices }`, arena/index based, face = surface + sense, coedge with optional pcurve, vertex and edge tolerances. Conceptually close, which is the one thing that makes a translation plausible. Differences that would bite: their tolerance model is global (`Kernel` carries zone tolerances; our vertices/edges carry local `tol`), their face trimming is surface-cache based (`surface::trim_srf_cache`), ours is pcurve-based; their booleans/intersector and healing are SMLib's, ours are separate code.

Rough sizes in `crates/solidcore/src` (lines): blend 123.5k, topology 111.9k, surface 102.7k, nurbs 71.9k, intersect 56.2k, curve 53.0k, geom 44.7k, boolean 24.9k, offset 22.6k, heal 18.1k, total 687k. Within blend: fillet_solver ~15k, fillet_geom ~21k, fillet_executive ~14k plus 5.7k session, fillet_corner ~10k, fillet_standard_solver ~7.5k, `fillet_section` 4.1k (interval arithmetic + certificate), `fillet_exact` 2.6k, `fillet_removal` 2.8k, `fillet_cliff_overflow` 2.1k, `fillet_overflow` 1.2k, `blend_setback` 2.1k.

Portability, by piece:
- *Algorithmic and mostly standalone (candidates to reimplement):* the exact rolling-ball classes (`fillet_exact`, Dahl and Krasauskas, needs only conics/NURBS rational helpers); the G^n section generator (`fillet_section/{form,section,series}.rs`, ~1.8k lines, depends on `Vec3`, B-spline curves and `Kernel` only in `certificate.rs`/`station.rs`); setback fill (`blend_setback.rs`, planes only, uses `nurbs::b_spline_surface`); the fold/room conditions (small closed forms); the refusal-with-size idea.
- *Tied to their types (not portable as code):* the executive/session, solver trace, corner topology builders, removal/cleanup, overflow trim. They manipulate `Brep`, `EdgeId`, `Kernel`, surface-cache and intersector objects throughout, with upstream status codes (`SM_ERR_*`, 1001) preserved for difftest equality.
- Dependencies of the whole path: all of nurbs, surface, curve, intersect, topology and heal. A faithful translation of the corner code would drag in their SSI intersector (`solidcore-ssi`, `ssi`) too.
- Maturity: version 0.0.x in README (crate says 1.0.1 to track upstream), "API is experimental"; 4,186 differential cases still deferred behind unported stubs. Blend units that are still partial: `fillet_corner_p3` (126 deferred), `fillet_geom_p6` (78), `fillet_executive_p3` (46). Whether the Rust `fillet_edges` path matches the C++ on our cases: not verified.

## 4. Would it solve our failures?

Judged from code and their test lists only; nothing run.

| Our failure | What SolidCore/SMLib does | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| (1) vertex with 2-3 filleted edges: `branching blend selection`, `concave chain end is not on a planar face` | A classified corner layer: End, Join, Turn (mitre), Ball, Setback; plus 3x2 mixed and concave classes. Box corners, three-edge chamfers, pocket and boss mitres, tee of unequal pipes, mixed 3x2 at a peg pass in their rig. Y-junction of concave rounds refused; corners on curved faces with setback/G2 not yet. | Likely fixes the planar and mildly curved corners. | (2) chains with a crease or ending on a cone/cylinder/torus neighbour | End event builds `S ∩ E` on the end face; passes: bar edge ending on a cylindrical boss, a round into a cylinder, tangent chains over an earlier round's end. The decisive check is to run both PET and daisy bodies through SolidCore. Proposal (not done): on the CI box (alias in the local ssh config; first `uptime` and `pgrep -fc "vitest|cargo|rustc"`, and only if idle), in a fresh scratch directory outside our repo: clone solidcore at the pinned commit, export each failing body as STEP from our kernel, import with `crates/step-import`/`step-brep` into a `Brep`, call `blend::fillet_executive::api::fillet_edges` for the failing edges, and record status, volume against an analytic or OCCT reference, and closedness (`topology::brep::p2::is_manifold_solid`). One temporary harness binary, no change to either repo's source, no host, user or path in any committed file. That answers (1) to (3) directly; I would not decide on integration before seeing it. Expected cost: half a day including the STEP round-trip fidelity questions (their issue list shows STEP-read bodies failing where native builds pass).

## 5. Recommendation

Do not port. Do not take SolidCore as a dependency for fillets either: the blend code needs the whole kernel, SolidCore's own status shows it is still being wired together, and swapping kernels is a different decision from fixing blends. Reimplement the useful algorithms in our own `ck-ops/src/blend` over our own `ck-topo`/`ck-geom`, using SMLib's design as the reference and the papers behind it, with an Apache-2.0 attribution entry for whatever we derived from the code.

The problem is as much structural as it is algorithmic. We build each blend as a tool solid and boolean it. That is why corners are mitres where two tools meet (`cornerNotTangent` warnings), why concave chain ends need a planar neighbour, and why overflow becomes a refusal. SMLib's gains come from trimming on the rails and treating corners as events of one trace. Making the trace own the end/corner/overflow events is the lesson to take; it can be done incrementally.

Staged plan (effort is rough, one engineer; risk in brackets):

0. *Evidence* (0.5-1 day, low): the CI-box experiment above on PET petals and the daisy; also run the failing SolidCore cases we care about to see their refusal text. Decide with data.
1. *Refusals with a size* (2-3 days, low): replace `foot 1` / `blendOverflow` messages with the fold/room closed forms (Lukacs conditions) and a "largest size that builds" value in the structured error (we already have `blendTooLargeMax`, `filletShape`). Pure win for users even if nothing else lands.
2. *Cliff overflow* (1-2 weeks, medium): build the blend on the faces continued past the rim, then trim on the crease with the neighbour (line on a cylinder, conic on a cone for plane neighbours), instead of refusing. Our tool-boolean construction can do this by extending the tool and letting the neighbour cut it; needs tests from their cases (disc rim past a thin wall, hole rim past a plate).
3. *Corner events for 2 and 3 edges* (3-5 weeks, high): the End/Turn(mitre)/Ball classification in one place, generalising `corner.rs`, `mixed.rs`, `vertex.rs` to curved neighbours (cylinder, cone, torus) by solving the corner ball on the offsets rather than assuming planes. This is where the PET petals live. Setback with a convex-certified fill is a later extension (2-3 more weeks, high) and needs planes first, as in SolidCore.
4. *Tangent-end and end-on-cone/torus events* (2-3 weeks, medium): the end rib built outward from the tangent point rather than marched into it; fixes chains that die at a seam.
5. *Narrow wedges* (open-ended, high): no reference solution found. Options: refuse with a size (step 1), or a spine-continuation past the tip (the SolidCraft `KNIFE` idea) tested first on the daisy. Do not promise this one.

Risks: (a) licence decision on our side is open (no licence file seen in the kernel `main`); (b) SolidCore's Rust blend may not reproduce the C++ on our bodies (4,186 deferred differential cases, three blend units partial) so the experiment may show its limits rather than SMLib's; (c) their corpus is not ours: nothing in it is a tori/cone petal, so wins on their list do not transfer by default; (d) the generic-blend rework is the fork's work, the Rust port is incremental and opt-in (`UpstreamFixes`); (e) STEP round-trip may introduce failures unrelated to blending; (f) wasm build size and stack (8 MiB link argument) matter if we ever consider embedding it.

Alternative that stays on the table: do nothing from them and keep improving our engine on the failing corpus (`tools/blend_corpus.mjs`, `docs/blend-corpus.md`). Steps 1 and 2 above are that path and are cheap enough to do regardless of the experiment's result.
