# Changelog

Versions follow `MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH`; the live site's `version.json` carries the commit it was built from. Dates are when the change went live. Write each entry as a bold title and one or two short sentences: the What's new page shows the title and the first sentence.

## Unreleased

## 0.6.0 - 2026-11-11

- **The Washer wizard can put a washer on a hole.** Click "Washer on a hole", then a hole's wall or circular edge: the wizard picks the standard size whose ISO 273 clearance hole fits, and seats the washer coaxial on the face the hole opens on (the counterbore floor for a counterbored hole). Countersunk holes are refused with a message.
- **An arc slot can be edited after it is drawn.** Click one of its arcs in the sketch and change its radius, start angle, sweep or width in the fields that appear; the whole slot follows and stays tangent, and a radius or width you typed as a dimension keeps its dimension.
- **The guide says CivilKit CAD, has the suite navigation, and covers this release.** The guide's text now uses the product's name, its menu bar links to Calcs, Calculators, CAD, Guides and What's new, and the new features (BOM as text, Delete both, Enter repeat, woods and grain, folds across a window, a flange on a flange's free end) are described.
- **A flange or hem can go on the free end of a flange (a double fold).** Clicking the edge at the tip of an existing flange now adds a second flange or a hem there; before, the app refused with "edge already has a flange".
- **Woods show a grain.** A part with a solid wood material (walnut, oak, birch and the rest) now shows growth rings and fine streaks in a darker tone of its colour, along the longest side of the part; MDF, chipboard and OSB stay plain.
- **A fold can cross a window in the sheet.** A fold line that runs through a hole or window of a sheet metal panel now bends the part and cuts the window through the bend; before, it was refused with "a hole of the panel meets the bend zone".
- **A plain extrude cut through a folded sheet keeps it a sheet.** A through-all (or deep enough) Extrude cut drawn parallel to the sheet is now a sheet cut, so the part still unfolds; a shallow pocket still ends the sheet.
- **A sketch on the plate survives removing the only fold.** The plate's faces keep working under both of their names, so a slot drawn on the plate no longer loses its reference when you remove the fold; fold refusals also say why (a line across both arms of a U) and what to change.
- **Enter repeats the last command; the view cube's edges and corners are bigger.** With nothing in progress (no sketch, no feature being edited, no dialog), Enter runs **Repeat last command** again. The cube in the corner of the view has deeper bevels, so its edges and corners are easier to hit and its faces are smaller.
- **Deleting a feature that others use offers to delete them too.** When a delete is blocked, the notice now names the features that use it and has a **Delete both** button that removes them together in one step (one Undo brings them back).
- **Eight more woods and three wood panels in the Material list.** Birch, cedar, cherry, Douglas fir, eucalyptus, maple, teak and walnut join Oak and Pine, each with density and stiffness from a named source and its own colour; chipboard P5, OSB/3 and birch plywood join MDF, marked unverified until checked against the product standards.
- **The bill of materials copies and saves as plain text.** The assembly BOM gains **Copy as text** and **Save as text** (aligned columns, ready for an e-mail); every CSV the app writes (BOM, frame cut list, drawing tables) now ends rows with CR LF, and text that starts with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` is written so a spreadsheet shows it as text, not a formula.
- **The parts library is credited as BOSL2-based parts.** The library formerly named "BOSL2 parts" is now **BOSL2-based parts**, with a credit line (BOSL2 by Revar Desmera and contributors, BSD-2-Clause, link) on its row in the Libraries tab, in each part's detail panel, in the guide and in `THIRD_PARTY.md`.
- **A wheel zoom speed setting.** Settings > Navigation gains **Wheel zoom speed** (0.5x to 3x, 1x as before) for the mouse wheel and trackpad pinch, in the 3D view and in a sketch; it is remembered on the device.
- **An agent can see and drive the part you have open (agent link).** Settings gains **Agent link**: paste the one-time code an MCP assistant gets from `bridge_pair` and it can read the open part (`app_state`), take a picture of it (`app_screenshot`) and, only if you tick **Allow the agent to change this document**, run scripts on it as one undo step (`app_run_script`, `app_undo`, `app_redo`). It is off at every start, works only on this computer, shows an **Agent: linked** item in the status bar and a notice for each change; closing the page ends it. The in-app chat is not part of this yet. Guide: Agents and the MCP server.
- **A Solarized Dark theme.** Settings > Theme gains **Solarized Dark**, a dark theme in the Solarized palette (panels, text, accents and the viewport background); it is remembered like the other themes.
- **OBJ export keeps the body names and colours.** Exporting OBJ now writes one object per body, named as in the tree, and a body with a colour carries it on its vertices (`x y z r g b`, read by Blender, MeshLab and most viewers); hidden bodies are left out as before when *visible only* is on.
- **A misspelt agent-tool field is refused with a suggestion.** A tool call with an unknown field (`veiw` for `view`) fails before anything runs, naming the closest allowed field, listing the allowed ones and saying that parameters go at the top level of the input.
- **Pipe cut says what it does.** Its editor now notes that it rolls a ball along the picked edges and leaves a surface body, and points to Hole (or a sketch and a cut) for a round hole. After the cut the app says when the solid became a surface body, and when no face changed at all.
- **A Washer wizard for flat and spring washers.** Pick a standard size from a filterable list (ISO 7089, ISO 7093-1, DIN 127 B) or type your own, then place it like a screw; the result is one sketch and one extrude in a *Washer* folder, so it patterns and mirrors. The spring washer is a flat split ring (no lift), and `washer({ inner, outer, thickness, kind, gap, origin, axis })` does the same in the Script tab.

## 0.5.0 - 2026-11-10

- **SolidWorks sheet metal parts open as editable sheet metal.** An SLDPRT sheet metal part now opens with its sheet metal history read back: its edge flanges come back as editable **Edge flange** features with their outline, including flanges that cover only part of an edge or follow a sketched profile, and the leg rounds, slots and round holes of the sheet are kept. Check the result against the SolidWorks model: unusual features are left out rather than guessed
- **Edge flanges and folds on a base with rounded corners.** A sheet metal base with arcs in its outline (filleted corners, slots at the edge) no longer refuses an edge flange; the flange is built on the straight edges and the arcs stay as they are
- **The app shows its version, and releases are cut with a script.** Settings now ends with "Version X.Y.Z (app <commit>, kernel <build>)", and the live site's `version.json` carries the same three values (version, app commit, kernel build). Releases are numbered `MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH` from `package.json`; `node tools/release.mjs minor` moves these notes under a dated heading, tags the kit and kernel repos with the same version and prints the notes (see `docs/releasing.md`)
- **One filter for the Library: categories, tags and counts.** The Library picker and the Catalog's Libraries tab now share one filter bar: the search box (title, category, tags), a Category menu with the number of parts in each, tag chips for the parts shown (click several to require all of them; twelve at first, **More tags** for the rest) and **Clear filters**. Every bundled sample now has one of twelve groups (Fasteners, Power transmission, Motors & electronics, Pipe & fluid, Fans & impellers, Mechanisms, Brackets & structure, Enclosures, Sheet metal, Consumer products, 3D printing, Marine) and two to five tags, so "Fasteners" plus "threaded" finds the threaded bolts at once. A library you add keeps its own category text, and a recognised one ("tubing & pipe", "bearings", "sheet metal"...) is also filed under the matching group; categories with fewer than three parts fold into Other in the menu only. The last category and tags are remembered per place on the device. With Fasteners chosen (or the tag bearing), **Make one with the Screw wizard / Bearing wizard** opens that wizard. Guide: The Library.
- **Insert a part from the Library picker.** Each part card in the Library (bundled samples and added libraries) now has **Insert into this part** under it, next to the default click that opens the part as a new document. It closes the picker and arms the part exactly like clicking it in the Catalog's Libraries tab: click a face or plane of the open part to place it there (Placement options and Esc as before), one undo step. The button is greyed out when no document is open, and a library whose licence is not confirmed yet asks first.
- **The Samples button is now Library, with every library in one picker, and a Manage libraries list.** The top-bar **Samples** button and its dialog are renamed **Library**. The dialog lists the bundled samples and the parts of every library you added, grouped by library, under the same filter box, with the **Recent** row above; clicking a part opens it as a new document (a library whose licence is not confirmed yet asks first). **Manage libraries**, in the picker and in the Catalog's Libraries tab, lists each library with **Disable / Enable** (a disabled library is hidden from the picker and the Catalog but kept), **Refresh** and **Remove**, and **+ Add library** (the same preview and licence confirmation as before). The Catalog's Libraries tab still adds a part to the open document by drag or click-then-face; the picker still opens a whole part. Cards of saved parts now show the part's own saved picture: a file saved by the app carries a small `thumbnail` field, and a library part that has one shows it (when it still matches the part) instead of the placeholder. The Recent row already showed it for files you saved or opened.
- **Edge flanges and hems on part of an edge, and shaped legs, in the editor.** The Sheet tab's **Edge flange** editor gains **Width** (the whole edge, *Start and length* along the picked edge, or *Between two points* typed or picked with **Pick two points** in the view), **Shape** (*Rectangle*, or *Sketch outline*: pick a sketch, or press **New sketch for the outline** to get a sketch on a plane through the flange, drawn in the flange's own frame with the default leg as a rectangle, and the sketcher opens on it; circles inside the outline are holes), **Holes** (list circles by centre and radius, or tick the circles of a sketch) and **Relief** (none, the rule's rectangle or your own size, for a flange that stops inside its edge). The **Hem** editor gets the width (from a start, or two points) and the relief. A flange imported from SolidWorks opens with its two points, its outline (shown as *Imported outline*) and its holes in these fields, and keeps them when another field is edited; **Convert to an editable sketch** turns an imported outline into a sketch of the same lines, arcs and circles (outlines that start beside the flange stay imported, and the form says why). The edge chip of an imported flange shows its point instead of staying blank. The script API (`edgeFlange` / `hem`: `start`, `length`, `from`, `to`, `profile: 'sketch'`, `holes`, `relief`), the MCP `sheet_reference` tool, the agent guide and the repair hints describe the same fields. The kernel cannot cut a shaped leg whose two span points run against the edge's direction (it reports "the profile does not reach beyond the bend"); the editor builds the flange both ways when it makes a sketch and keeps the one that works.
- **Dimension a sketch to the part's own edge in one click.** With the Dimension tool you can now click an outer edge of the part (a body edge, not sketch geometry), for example to set a slot's distance from the plate's side: the edge is projected into the sketch as fixed reference geometry automatically (once; a second dimension to the same edge reuses it) and the dimension is made, where before it said "Nothing to pick there". An edge square-on to the sketch plane says why it cannot be used, and the message for an empty click is clearer.
- **An Arc slot sketch tool.** The Slot button in the sketch tools now also offers **Arc slot** (in its flyout): a curved slot made of two concentric arcs joined by two half-round ends. Click the arc centre, click the start point on the centre line of the slot (this sets the radius and the start angle), click where the arc ends (it follows the pointer round, either way), then click or type the width. Typed radius and width are held as dimensions; the curved sides stay tangent to the rounded ends. It extrudes and cuts like any other closed profile.
- **Edge flange, Hem and Corner open with nothing selected.** The three Sheet commands no longer stay grey until an edge or corner is picked: they open their editor with the edge (or corner) list already active, and each tap adds one or takes it out, with no Cmd or Shift, so several can be chosen on a phone. Picks made before still go in.
- **Sheet metal: Convert to sheet, Cut across bends, Corner round and chamfer, and panel picks.** The Sheet tab's Create group gains **Convert to sheet** (turns a bent plate solid into a sheet with its flat pattern; the thickness is read from the body unless you give it, with a button for the active rule's thickness, and a rule list for the k-factor), **Cut** (cuts the closed regions of a sketch through the flat pattern, across bends too: tick the regions in Shapes or click them in the view; the flat pattern and the DXF show the hole across the bend on the inner cut layer) and **Corner** (rounds or chamfers the corners you select in vertex mode: chips with **Pick in view**, a Round or Chamfer choice that keeps the size, radius or distance; the default is a round of three thicknesses). Each has an icon, a ribbon tooltip, a command search entry, a tree name and an editor; a refused feature shows the kernel's own reason ("the corner of panel P0 touches the bend side e1") with advice. On a sheet with more than one panel the **Edge flange** and **Hem** editors add an edge of any panel (its free end, a side or a wall by name, or an edge by number: `P2.tip`, `P1 edge 3`) and the **Fold** editor a **Panel** list. A converted solid counts as the sheet's first plate (flanges, hems, folds, cuts and corners work on it; base and contour flange are off). Fixed: the flat pattern, DXF and bend table could describe the wrong feature when a later feature's id sorted before an earlier one's (`E1` before `F3`): the sheet entries are now in feature order. Needs the kernel with sheet convert, cut and corner. Guide: docs/sheet-metal.md.
- **Selecting a component in the feature tree lights it up in the viewport.** Clicking a component row (an inserted part) highlights all of that component's faces and edges, so one of many near-identical parts can be told apart; the highlight clears when another row is selected or an editor opens, and picked faces and edges are lit as before.
- **Four sheet metal samples.** The sample list has four parts made with the new Sheet metal tools, each with its feature tree to read: a plate with an edge flange and a chained second bend, a hem, a hole cut through the flat pattern, then unfolded and refolded; a box with a flange on every edge, round corner reliefs and chamfered leg corners; a hat channel made with a contour flange; and a plate folded along two lines.
- **A Sheet metal tab.** The new **Sheet metal** ribbon tab (also in the command search and the tool palette) has four groups: **Rules** (one rules editor: thickness, k-factor, bend radius, relief width and depth, corner relief and shape, gaps; a field may be `=Thickness*2`), **Create** (Base flange from a closed sketch, Edge flange on picked edges with height, angle, radius, height reference, position, flip and chained bends, Contour flange from an open chain of lines, Hem, Fold along a sketch line), **Flatten** (Unfold, Refold, and a **Flat pattern** panel with the outline, holes, bend lines with angles, the blank's size and a row per bend, which follows the open editor's draft) and **Export** (flat pattern DXF with version, units, layer, tangent line and bend note options, and the bend table as CSV or JSON). Each feature has its own editor with live preview, a tree icon and name; a part opens on the tab when it gets its first plate. Needs the kernel with sheet metal. guide: docs/sheet-metal.md.
- **A reference that no longer resolves says what is there instead.** When a feature uses a face or an edge that an earlier edit removed, the feature's status now names the missing face and lists the faces of that feature that exist now (closest name first), or, for an edge between two faces that no longer meet, the faces the first one meets. Fixing the reference is a one-field edit. Needs the kernel with `candidates` on `missingReference` and `missingEdge`.
- **Script: colour faces by role, not by feature id.** `p.appearance({ face: handle, only: ['S.e3', 'C1'], colour })` colours just those faces of the feature the handle names: `S.e3` is the side made by sketch entity e3, `C0` / `C1` the caps, `B*` the blend faces. Scripts no longer need hand-written ids such as `F32.S.e1`, which pointed at the wrong feature when a step was added above them.
- **US and European steel stock for Frames, and steel grades in the Material editor.** The Frame **Profile** list gains the US W, S, C, MC and L shapes, the full set of HSS and pipe (1,087 US profiles, the earlier HSS ids kept) and the European IPE, IPE A/O/AA, HE A/B/M/AA, IPN and UPN sections with EN 10210 hot-finished and EN 10219 cold-formed SHS, RHS and CHS (1,086 EU profiles), each with its standard, grade, designation (US: imperial and metric), mass per metre and source. A **Region** list picks the stock, and the regional lists are loaded only when used: the program itself is about 300 kB smaller than before (3,320 to 3,022 kB) and the three lists are separate downloads of 11, 39 and 26 kB compressed; a file with a frame of a regional profile loads its region when it opens. Each profile names its default grade (A992, A36, A500 Gr C, A53, S355, S355J2H; **default, confirm**), shown in the bill of materials and used for the part's material when you create a frame on a part with no material of its own. The Material editor lists fifteen steel grades (EN 10025-2, EN 10210/10219, ASTM A36, A992, A572, A53, A500, AS/NZS 3679.1) by region and standard, with ultimate strength, shear modulus, thermal expansion, standard, grade and region; grades with a single secondary source or conflicting editions wear an **unverified** badge. Existing files and material names are unchanged. The AISC workbook came from a public redistribution because aisc.org refused the download; reuse terms are to review (THIRD_PARTY.md).
- **Australian and US steel stock for Frames.** The Frame **Profile** list now has 682 catalogue profiles on top of the generic ones, grouped by region, standard and kind of section, with a search box: Australian hollow sections (AS/NZS 1163 SHS, RHS and CHS, grades C250L0, C350L0, C450L0), rolled steel (AS/NZS 3679.1 UB, UC, UBP, PFC, equal and unequal angles, flat and round bar) and US ASTM A500 Grade B/C HSS with imperial names and the metric size. Each profile has its published mass per metre, so the cut list and bill of materials mass match the catalogue, and the bill of materials gains standard, grade and total mass columns (also in the CSV). I sections and channels use the new `ibeam` and `channel` (own flange and web thickness, root radius) sections; they mitre, butt and tee but offer no connectors. Sources, checks and what was left out: guide, Frame stock data and sources.
- **Import SVG as sketch geometry.** **Import SVG into sketch** and **New sketch from SVG** (Home > Sketch, the command search, and dropping an `.svg` file on the window) bring a vector drawing into a sketch, next to the DXF ones. Paths (lines, arcs, cubic and quadratic curves, relative or absolute), rectangles (also rounded), circles, ellipses, lines, polylines and polygons are read, inside groups and under any transform. Lines, circular arcs, circles and ellipses stay exact (also after a rotation or mirror); Bezier curves become splines that follow the curve; closed outlines close, so they extrude, and an inner outline cuts a hole. The size comes from the file's width, height and viewBox in mm, cm, in or pt (a file without one is read at 96 dpi, with a notice), and the drawing is turned upright. Text, images, fills, strokes, gradients and scripts are not read and are listed in a notice; a file that declares entities, a file over 10 MB, more than 20,000 entities and coordinates beyond 1,000,000 mm are refused.
- **Dimension between centre lines and centre marks.** In a drawing, the dashed centre lines of shafts and the centre marks of holes are now real objects: Select highlights just the centre line instead of the whole view, and the Dimension tool accepts them, so a click on two shaft axes gives the distance between them (outside the view, draggable like any dimension), a centre line and an edge the distance to it, two centre marks the distance between the hole centres, and two centre lines that meet the angle. The dimensions follow the part, the view and the scale, survive saving, and appear in the PDF, SVG and DXF. The hint line now reads Pick an edge or centre line.
- **3D Sketch panel restyled, and an Introduction to 3D sketching.** The panel now fits the narrow left panel: the five draw modes are one grid of icon buttons with a short hint under them, the form is split into **Draw**, **Where you click**, **Add by coordinates**, **Points** (a compact table with X, Y, Z in one row and a bin button) and **Mesh**, **Cancel** and **Create** stay in a bar at the bottom while the form scrolls, and a **?** button shows a short reminder with a link to the guide. A new guide page, Introduction to 3D sketching, walks through a pipe swept along a rising spline and a curve fitted to an imported mesh.
- **Script assertions: `expectMinFaceArea`, a tolerance option, and per-body checks.** `P.expectMinFaceArea(a)` fails when any face is smaller than `a` mm2 (no slivers) and names the smallest face. `expectVolume(v, { tolerance })` (relative, default 0.1 %) and `expectBBox(box, { tolerance })` (absolute mm, default 0.001) take `{ body: 'Name' }` to check one named body instead of all of them, and a failure reads `expected volume 48000, got 47961.37 (tolerance 1e-6 relative)`. A lofted body trimmed by a cylinder reads its true box (not the control polygon). The Tapered disc and curved-petal PET bottle samples carry checks
- **Draw curves from a function in a script.** `s.curve((t) => [x, y], { from, to, count, closed })` samples a JavaScript function into one fitted spline (`closed: true` for a full loop that does not repeat its end point, ready as an extrude profile or a loft section) and `s.polar((angle) => radius, { count })` draws a polar curve (waves, cams, petals, rounded stars; the angle is in radians). The scripting docs and the guide have examples (a closed spline as a loft section, a wavy outline). The Tapered disc sample now draws its wave sections with `s.polar`: same 21 features, volume 78,971 mm3 (it was 78,968, the sections are now closed splines instead of open ones that return to their start)
- **Script checks show as pass / fail rows.** After a Run the Script tab lists every `expectVolume`, `expectBBox`, `expectMinFaceArea`, `expectValid` and `expectFaceCount` check with a tick and what was checked, or a cross and the failure (also in the red banner), plus a green line with the number of features built. `s.curve` also takes `t0`, `t1` and `n` for `from`, `to` and `count`, and `s.polar` takes `n`. `expectBBox` reads the display box (the mesh joined with the edge curves), documented with its tolerance
- **Script errors are impossible to miss, and typing in the Script box stays in the box.** A failed run shows a red banner above the code box with the message and line number (it stays until the next Run) and tints the failing line; declaring a name the script already owns (`const plane = ...`) now says that the name is a script helper and suggests a rename, and the reserved names are listed in the scripting docs. Opening the Script tab focuses the box, Run and Cancel hand the focus back to it, and the viewport's single-key shortcuts no longer fire while the Script tab shows and focus is anywhere but the viewport (after a tab switch, Fit or a cancelled prompt), nor from any editor or text field
- **New sample: Tapered spinning disc.** A flat disc that slopes up from a thin edge to a low cone at the centre, with 12 smooth ridges running from the centre to a plain circular edge that rises and falls once per ridge (so a ball rolling off it drops into a tube). It is one lofted body through wave-shaped spline sections, trimmed by a cylinder; variables N (ridges), K (ridge depth), R (radius) and T0 (minimum edge thickness), and POCKET = 1 hollows the underside with a round recess (about half the material) for a lighter print.
- **PET bottle with curved petal grooves, and a rounded groove floor on the straight-groove bottle.** A new sample, **PET bottle, curved petals**, sweeps a round groove along a 3D spline (a 3D Sketch path) for each foot gap: it starts in the push-up cone, runs under the standing ring, round the base corner and up the side wall, fading out in it, with an optional twist (SWIRL); the cavity is cut by the same paths one wall wider so the wall stays even. The straight-groove script gains TIP (rounded groove tips) and PETAL_FILLET (rounded groove floor, made by rounding each cutting tool because the kernel cannot yet fillet the groove edges on the base itself: docs/pet-petal-fillet.md lists which edge pairs fail and why).
- **A 3D Sketch tool.** **3D Sketch** (Home > Sketch, and the command search) draws lines, 3-point arcs, splines, control-vertex splines and points in space: click on the ground or a work plane (with a height above it, or typed X, Y, Z), snapping to model corners, edge ends and middles and other sketch points; drag a point's diamond, delete points, undo a point, and double-click the sketch in the tree to edit it. The curves now show in the 3D view (they did not before). A 3D sketch holding one spline is offered as the path of **Sweep** and **Path pattern**, and the tool can add a plane normal to the path's start for the profile. Constraints are not offered; guide curves and Project curve still take flat sketches only.
- **Chamfer chains end flush and mitre cleanly; more SolidWorks features rebuilt.** A chamfer along a chain of edges now ends flush with the neighbouring faces and mitres at the corners, and the SolidWorks history rebuild reaches further on more parts: surface faces of a solid are read correctly, extrudes are rebuilt where the file's sketch is ambiguous, blends are regenerated, and a degenerate (NaN) value no longer stops a rebuild. Some parts that opened as one imported body now open with their features, and some that opened with a partial history keep more of it.
- **The feature history of an imported SolidWorks part sits in one folder you can switch off.** A `.SLDPRT` with any rebuilt history, complete or partial, now opens with every rebuilt feature (planes, sketches, extrudes, fillets ...) inside one folder of the feature tree, "SolidWorks history", and the eye on the folder row switches the whole folder off or on with one click (one undo step). Off, the part is exactly the file's body; on, a complete history gives the same body (same volume as the plain import) and a partial one gives the rebuilt features followed by the rest of the file as one imported body. For a complete history the part also holds the file's body as an "Imported body" step that is suppressed while the folder is on and takes over when it is off. A history the kernel can only rebuild part of used to be thrown away for one "Imported STEP body"; it is now kept: the rebuilt prefix, then an "Imported remainder" step (the rest of the file, replacing the rebuilt body with the file's own), and the opening notice says how many features were rebuilt and that the rest is imported, so edits to the rebuilt features will not change the body. A folder can now be one feature with its sketch (two features), and its row counts the sketches. Both folder states are regenerated before the part is used and must come out at the file's volume (0.5%); anything else still falls back to the plain import.
- **Split Entity: break a line, arc, circle or fit spline at a picked point.** A new **Split entity** tool in the sketch Edit group (with Trim and Extend; a tap on a touch screen works like a click) breaks the curve under the pointer into two that meet at one shared point, so a closed outline stays closed and extrudes exactly as before (a rectangle side, a half-disc arc and a disc each give the same volume to 6 digits, checked on the kernel). Picking a sketch point that lies on the curve uses it as the joint; picking an end is refused. A circle becomes two arcs about the same centre (from the picked place to the opposite side and back). Constraints that mentioned the original go where they still hold: horizontal and vertical on both halves (a slanted line gets a Parallel between its halves), angle, perpendicular, radius, diameter and the rest stay on the first piece, tangent goes to the piece that holds the end it touches, and a dimension across the whole line still reads true. What cannot carry (equal and midpoint on a line, whose length changed; anything on a spline) is dropped, and the status line names the dropped kinds. The joint slides along the curve, so the sketch gains one degree of freedom. A fit spline is re-fitted as two fit splines through the same points plus the joint: the shape follows the old curve closely (0.06% in area on a gentle arch) but not exactly, since each half now has a natural end at the joint (1.5% when cut on a data point at the top of the arch; 7% when a closed spline is cut open), and the hint says so. Control-point splines and ellipses are not split. Undo takes the split back in one step.
- **A Project curve command.** Features > Reference > **Project curve** projects a sketch, or an earlier Edit / Project curve selected in the tree, onto the faces you picked (none picked: every face of the part) as one smooth curve lying on the surface, for a groove path or a guide curve. The editor has the source, the faces (Use selection), the direction (both ways, along or against the sketch normal), the degree (2 to 9, flagged before the kernel is asked) and the fit tolerance. The sketch's own Project tool is a different thing and is unchanged.
- **Edit curve and Freeform commands.** **Edit curve** (Features > Reference) rebuilds the picked edges, the curves of a sketch or an earlier curve as one curve: by number of control points or by tolerance, with keep ends, end tangents and corners, flattening onto a plane, and a list of control point edits (point number, offset along the world axes or along the curve, weight, skip). It starts by tolerance with corners kept, so a sketch with a corner is not refused. **Freeform** (Surfaces > Edit) rebuilds one picked face as a net of control points (degree and spans, 5 x 5 points by default) and lists point moves (points as column,row, offset along the world axes, the face or a plane, spread to neighbours, mirror about a plane). Not done: dragging control points in the viewport (the offsets are typed), adding edges to an Edit curve after it is made, and the net's `of` mapping.
- **Feature commands doc and creator header no longer say Scale and Move face have no command.** Scale has its own command (Features > Modify) and Move face / Move body come from the positioning ball; the stale note that the kernel answers `TODO` for them is gone, and the list of features still without a command is corrected.

- **Live parameters keep `min`, `max`, `pow`, `mod`, `round` and the other functions.** The expression grammar of a part gained `min`, `max`, `pow`, `mod`, `hypot`, `atan2`, `asin`, `acos`, `atan`, `round`, `floor`, `ceil`, `trunc`, `exp`, `ln` and `log10` (called as `name(...)`, with a comma between arguments), and an imported SolidCraft file whose parameters use them now stays live instead of keeping the number. Expressions that were valid read as before, and a variable may still be called `max` or `round`.
- **SolidCraft import keeps parameters live.** The named parameters of a converted file used to be evaluated once and their names dropped. They now arrive as the part's variables, and an extrude depth, a hole diameter or depth, a fillet radius, a chamfer or shell thickness, a sphere radius, a cylinder height, a revolve angle, a sweep twist, a pattern spacing or a sketch dimension that is written over them stays an expression: change a variable and the part follows. An expression the part cannot hold (functions beyond `sqrt`, `sin`, `cos`, `tan` and `abs`, or a parameter whose name the part cannot take) stays the number it evaluates to, and the import row says so.
- **SolidCraft import: parameter expressions with functions, powers and units.** A file whose parameters use `sqrt`, `sin`, `cos`, `min`, `max`, `pow`, `mod`, `round` and the like, a power (`a ^ 2`), `pi`, or a unit other than mm (`2 cm`, `1 in`, `0.5 rad`) was refused as a whole ("a parameter cannot be evaluated"). Such expressions are now evaluated: lengths are converted to millimetres and angles to degrees, trigonometric functions take degrees and the inverse ones return degrees. `log` and `sign` are still refused, because their base and their value at zero are not stated in the format.
- **SolidCraft import: symmetric, two-sided and tapered extrudes.** A symmetric extent (the total distance centred on the sketch plane, or through all) became an unsupported row, a taper was dropped without a word (the section came out straight), and a start from a face and an end offset from a face were ignored. A symmetric extent is now an extrude centred on the sketch plane, a second distance runs both distances from one extrude, a positive taper grows the section along the travel and a negative one shrinks it, a start from a face moves the profile to that face, and an end at a face works on a tilted sketch plane too. A taper combined with a symmetric or two-sided extent, an end offset from a face, and a second distance in the older per-side convention are reported on their row.
- **SolidCraft import: regions chosen by their whole outlines, and revolves about any axis.** Extrudes, revolves and sheet bases that name their regions by whole outer and hole outlines are carried (one curve of each outline picks the region; holes stay holes). A revolve about a line of its own sketch or a line in space that lies in the sketch plane, and a symmetric revolve, are carried; before, only the two sketch axes were.
- **SolidCraft import: sweeps along lines and arcs, lofts to a point and along rails.** A sweep along a path of lines and arcs in a planar sketch is carried, with its twist (the path sketch may draw other curves too: only the path's own are used). A profile kept at a fixed angle, an end scale and a guide rail on a sweep are reported instead of being dropped. A loft whose last or first section is a point (a cone or a pyramid) is carried, and so are up to two guide rails on a loft; a loft section that uses only some regions of its sketch is reported, where it used to take the whole sketch.
- **SolidCraft import: holes up to a face, circular patterns of features, mirrors of bodies, tori, sheet bases, ellipses and more constraints.** A hole drilled up to a face is a blind hole as deep as that face is far. A circular pattern of features about a coordinate axis through the origin is carried; a mirror of whole bodies (kept apart or united) too; a mirror and a pattern can seed one another. A torus, an ellipse in a sketch and a sheet-metal base flange (as a plate of the sheet thickness) are carried, and so are the parallel, perpendicular, equal, point-to-line distance and fixed-point constraints of a sketch.
- **SolidCraft import: sub-assemblies, hidden placements and timeline folders.** A component placed inside another component is inserted into that component's part and moves with it (it was placed from the design's origin, so a part inside a moved sub-assembly stood in the wrong place). A placement the file hides has its bodies hidden here too, which matters most for a part converted with its editable history kept hidden beside its final shape: the history no longer overlaps the shape. The timeline folders of the file become tree folders.
- **MCP `export` writes the stored view: `cache: true` on a `.ckpart`.** The file gets the same `viewCache` field as Save geometry in file, so the app shows the part at once on open. The view is built from the Session's regen reply by code now shared with the app (`@kit/viewdata`: view builder, key, codec). Refused with `view_unavailable`, writing nothing, for a curated or embedded font (text in the built-in fonts is cached), a rolled-back part, or a kernel whose wasm cannot be hashed. **The app says why it ignores a stored view**: edited part, different kernel build, unknown format version or unreadable field (no notice when the file has none).
- **Bearing rings have a ball groove.** Both rings of the Bearing wizard and `bearing()` now carry a real raceway: a circular arc in the section, centred on the ball circle at mid-width, slightly larger than the ball (radius 0.52 ball diameters) and 0.27 ball diameters deep, so the balls sit in it and the rings stand thicker at their sides, as in a deep-groove bearing. The groove is a true arc, so the faces stay clean for picking and sectioning, in open, shielded and sealed bearings alike. A bearing too small for a groove (a ring thinner than 0.4 mm under it, a narrow ring, or balls much smaller than the gap) keeps plain races. Outer diameter, bore and width are unchanged; ring volume and mass now include the groove.
- **A SolidWorks part opens as an editable body.** A `.sldprt` file used to appear as a view-only solid. The kernel reads the solid exactly (of 45 test parts 43 open, and wherever SolidWorks' own STEP of the part exists the volume agrees within 0.5% and the box is the same), and the app now writes that solid out as STEP, checks that a part built from it has the volume of the solid that was read, and opens the part with the body as its first feature, so it can be cut, drilled, filleted, measured and saved with the part. When the part cannot be built (the STEP cannot be written or the volume differs) the solid is shown view-only as before, with the old notice. The design history of the file is still not read.
- **A SolidCraft file converted from SolidWorks stands the way SolidWorks has it.** The importer turns every point of a file from Z up to this kit's Y up, but SolidWorks already is Y up with the front plane XY (as the `.sldprt` import and the `.ckpart` Dutchman writes keep it), so a converted part lay on its side (a bolt along +Y came out along -Z). The original solid a converted file keeps names the file it came from; when that is a `.sldprt` or `.sldasm`, nothing is turned: points, planes, directions and the original solid stay where the file has them, and the "(Y up)" turn is not added. Files from other programs are turned as before. The choice travels with each import, so imports can run side by side.
- **SolidCraft import checks the original solid of a converted file before it uses it.** A file converted from SolidWorks (or any program whose STEP the converter restates itself) keeps the original solid as STEP, and the converter can write some faces of it turned the other way: the kernel's importStep message repairs that, a part's importStep feature does not, so the same text gave a body of the wrong volume (a 10 mm cube shell came out at 859 mm3 instead of 424 mm3). The importer now asks the kernel to read the text both ways and, when they differ, uses the kernel's own healed STEP of it (the text is left alone when both agree, as for Fusion 360 files); a text the kernel cannot read is reported on the row and the sketches and operations are still carried. The same step checks a file that keeps its original solid *and* builds a body from sketches and operations: when the operations do not rebuild the stored solid (volume off by more than 0.5%, or errors), the stored solid is the body, the operations that build bodies are skipped with both volumes in the row, and the sketches stay.
- **A wizard's result lands in the feature tree as one folder named after the wizard.** Bearing, Screw, Knurl (cut and faceted forms), Propeller, Marine propeller, Turbo impeller and Gear (and Gear pair, mating gear included) stamp the cosmetic `group` on everything they add, through one shared helper (`wizardGroup.ts`), so the result can be hidden, shown, dragged or deleted as a unit (right-click the folder > Delete deletes exactly its features; a feature outside it that still uses one is handled by the usual dependency check). A cosmetic knurl is not a feature and has no folder. (#32)

The importer now reads that STEP text and writes it into the part as an `importStep` feature (the text sits in the part's `blobs` table, named by its SHA-256, so a large file stays out of the regeneration cache keys), turned from their Z-up to this kit's Y-up. - **The Display panel could not be scrolled to its last items.** Its height was capped as if the window had a short header, so with the full ribbon the bottom of the list (Grid, Relations, Overall size, Threads, Design issues) sat below the viewport, out of reach. The list now takes the room under its button, whatever the ribbon height, and scrolls inside it.

## 0.3.3 - 2026-10-26

### Changed
- **New defaults.** Reference planes are hidden until you switch them on (Show > Planes). A first visit now starts with the full ribbon, the left panel floating (with its Outliner / Features / Assets tabs), the inspector docked on the right, and no tool rail, selection mode bar, sketch tool palette or bottom drawer. Saved settings are untouched, and the three presets (Plasticity, Classic, Minimal) are unchanged.

## 0.3.2 - 2026-10-26

### Changed
- **Large parts open and edit much faster (kernel).** The kernel stored a snapshot of its state after every feature, which for a part with thousands of features meant copying a growing model thousands of times. A part longer than 256 features now stores about 256 evenly spaced snapshots. SELENE-showcase (4,218 features): the kernel's rebuild went from 24-39 s to about 7 s with identical output, and an edit near the end re-runs 53 features (1.3 s) instead of all of them. Parts of 256 features or fewer are unchanged.

## 0.3.1 - 2026-10-26

### Fixed
- **SolidCraft import: a profile corner on the sketch origin of a moved plane landed on the world origin.** SELENE's parabolic dish (a bowl profile on a plane at x = -190) revolved into a huge cone because its closing point went to (0, 0) instead of the plane's own origin; the same slip made `Extrude6` cut nothing and blocked five later features (6 errors, now 0). Any sketch of a file on a custom plane whose profile touches the sketch origin is affected.
- SELENE's path sketches (hose and wire routes) and coil helices are hidden on import like the other sketches and planes, instead of drawing as thin blue lines over the model.

## 0.3.0 - 2026-10-26

### Added
- **SolidCraft colours.** The importer reads each body's colour, opacity and (in newer files) its PBR values (metallic, roughness), and the bodies a file hides, and saves them in the part (`appearance`, `view`), keyed by the feature that made each body. SELENE now opens in its own colours instead of all grey, with its three hydraulic hoses hidden.
- **Metallic and roughness per body.** The Bodies panel has two sliders (Metallic, Roughness) next to Colour, Opacity and Finish. They override the finish, are saved with the part, and picking a finish or preset clears them. Not carried over: the file's index of refraction.
- **Bodies hidden by feature.** A feature id in the hidden list hides every body that feature made; showing one body keeps its siblings hidden.

### Fixed
- Bodies of an inserted component in an assembly are keyed `feature@insert` (e.g. `F2@F6`), so their colours and names no longer collide with the assembly's own feature of the same number; a component's bodies are named after the component.

## 0.2.2 - 2026-10-26

### Fixed
- **The feature tree scrolled into blank space on long parts** (SELENE, 4,218 features): the list looked for its scroll box in the wrong place once the left panel had tabs, so scrolling never loaded the rows. It now follows whichever box really scrolls.

## 0.2.1 - 2026-10-26

### Added
- **Syntax colouring in the Script box** (comments, strings, numbers, keywords, calls), in the light and dark themes.
- **Tabs can be dragged to reorder**, and a ▾ list of all open parts appears when they no longer fit (or there are many).
- **Keyboard shortcuts for tabs:** Ctrl+Tab / Ctrl+Shift+Tab step through them (also from the Script box), Ctrl+Alt+1..9 jump to one (9 is the last), Ctrl+Alt+T opens a new part, Ctrl+Alt+W closes the active one.

### Changed
- **Script panel:** the Examples list now sits on its own row above Run / Open / Save / Format, spans the panel, and has short names (it ran off the panel before); the examples added later no longer show their raw keys.
- **Switching tabs in the middle of a sketch finishes the sketch and keeps it** (as the check mark does) instead of refusing; when the sketch cannot be finished it stays open and says so.

## 0.2.0 - 2026-10-26

### Added
- **Document tabs.** Several parts open at once, as tabs under the top bar: each has its own undo history, file, unsaved-changes mark and camera. + opens a new part, × closes (a changed tab asks Save / Don't save / Cancel), opening a file or sample uses a blank tab or a new one and never asks about the part already open, and the page asks before it is left with any unsaved tab. See `docs/tabs.md`.
- **Autosave of every changed tab.** After a reload or crash the app offers to restore the session, each changed part in its own tab.

### Changed
- **Edit this part opens the component in its own tab** (linked to its assembly) instead of replacing the assembly on screen. Done writes it back as one undo step in the assembly's own history, and the assembly may now refuse a change that breaks a mate, with the part kept open.

## 0.1.2 - 2026-10-26

### Added
- **SolidCraft import: sweeps along a spline and coils.** The path sketch becomes a 3D sketch holding the spline; a coil becomes a helix, the wire's section and a sweep along it (round or square wire, either hand, inside, centred or outside). SELENE's hydraulic hoses, motor leads and suspension springs now come through: the showcase file imports with no unsupported feature (2,001 converted) and imports and rebuilds in about 25 seconds.

### Known

## 0.1.1 - 2026-10-26

### Fixed
- **Opening a big SolidCraft file looked dead.** Each fillet, chamfer or shell made the importer rebuild and validate the whole part built so far, minutes per question on a large file. It now rebuilds without meshing or validating and reuses the kernel's earlier states: the two SELENE files import in under a minute (35 s and 46 s) instead of many minutes.

### Added
- **Import progress.** A panel appears at once ("Importing X: feature n of m") with a Cancel button; an import that throws reports it; autosave waits while an import runs.

## 0.1.0 - 2026-10-25

First versioned release; this entry covers the last day of work.

### Added
- **Sphere feature.** A kernel `sphere` (centre and radius, one spherical face; add, cut or new body) with its editor and tree icon. The Catalog's solid sphere is now one row.
- **Bodies.** The Bodies panel shows the names a document gives, and offers Copy, Delete, Join, Cut and Intersect of whole bodies. Extrude, Revolve, Loft and Sweep have a New body operation with a name of its own. A failed body edit says why in words.
- **Frame planes.** A `plane` feature in `frame` mode: a plane given by an origin and two directions.
- **Installable app.** The studio installs as a PWA.
- **Stack status.** `npm run stack:status` compares the dev wasm, the built site and the live site with `main`; the pages build writes `version.json`.
- **SolidCraft import**
  - Holes (simple, drilled, counterbore, countersink, at a position or at sketch points) become our hole feature.
  - Sketches on tilted planes and cylinders along any axis import.
  - Circular patterns of bodies import as rotated copies about any axis.
  - Formats 31 to 33 read correctly: a leading `=` and decimal commas in expressions, `#n` face names, the counter of unnamed bodies, and `legacy_per_side` extents.

### Changed
- A SolidCraft body transfer that moves nothing is skipped instead of reported as unsupported.
- Kernel `ping` reports the commit the wasm was built from.

### Kernel (landed on `main` in the same period)
- Overhang-corner fillets generalise (mirror, under the ledge, two vertices; skewed corners are refused).
- Blade-fuse clips the hub-cylinder intersection to the blade rail when it overshoots.
- Read-only design-for-manufacture queries.
- Render close-up framing option.

### Known gaps in SolidCraft import
Splines, sweeps and coils; non-uniform scale; faithful body transfer (component ownership); joints are kept as a pose; tapped holes and threads.

### Gate (kernel regression suite, unchanged)
wire 280/280, valid.brep 280/319, vol.brep 319/319, mass.brep 271/319, naming.stable 214/216, step 16/21.
