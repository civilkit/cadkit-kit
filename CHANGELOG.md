# Changelog

Versions follow `MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH`; the live site's `version.json` carries the commit it was built from. Dates are when the change went live.

## Unreleased

- **Chamfer chains end flush and mitre cleanly; more SolidWorks features rebuilt.** A chamfer along a chain of edges now ends flush with the neighbouring faces and mitres at the corners, and the SolidWorks history rebuild reaches further on more parts: surface faces of a solid are read correctly, extrudes are rebuilt where the file's sketch is ambiguous, blends are regenerated, and a degenerate (NaN) value no longer stops a rebuild. Some parts that opened as one imported body now open with their features, and some that opened with a partial history keep more of it.
- **The feature history of an imported SolidWorks part sits in one folder you can switch off.** A `.SLDPRT` with any rebuilt history, complete or partial, now opens with every rebuilt feature (planes, sketches, extrudes, fillets ...) inside one folder of the feature tree, "SolidWorks history", and the eye on the folder row switches the whole folder off or on with one click (one undo step). Off, the part is exactly the file's body; on, a complete history gives the same body (same volume as the plain import) and a partial one gives the rebuilt features followed by the rest of the file as one imported body. For a complete history the part also holds the file's body as an "Imported body" step that is suppressed while the folder is on and takes over when it is off. A history the kernel can only rebuild part of used to be thrown away for one "Imported STEP body"; it is now kept: the rebuilt prefix, then an "Imported remainder" step (the rest of the file, replacing the rebuilt body with the file's own), and the opening notice says how many features were rebuilt and that the rest is imported, so edits to the rebuilt features will not change the body. A folder can now be one feature with its sketch (two features), and its row counts the sketches. Both folder states are regenerated before the part is used and must come out at the file's volume (0.5%); anything else still falls back to the plain import.
- **Split Entity: break a line, arc, circle or fit spline at a picked point.** A new **Split entity** tool in the sketch Edit group (with Trim and Extend; a tap on a touch screen works like a click) breaks the curve under the pointer into two that meet at one shared point, so a closed outline stays closed and extrudes exactly as before (a rectangle side, a half-disc arc and a disc each give the same volume to 6 digits, checked on the kernel). Picking a sketch point that lies on the curve uses it as the joint; picking an end is refused. A circle becomes two arcs about the same centre (from the picked place to the opposite side and back). Constraints that mentioned the original go where they still hold: horizontal and vertical on both halves (a slanted line gets a Parallel between its halves), angle, perpendicular, radius, diameter and the rest stay on the first piece, tangent goes to the piece that holds the end it touches, and a dimension across the whole line still reads true. What cannot carry (equal and midpoint on a line, whose length changed; anything on a spline) is dropped, and the status line names the dropped kinds. The joint slides along the curve, so the sketch gains one degree of freedom. A fit spline is re-fitted as two fit splines through the same points plus the joint: the shape follows the old curve closely (0.06% in area on a gentle arch) but not exactly, since each half now has a natural end at the joint (1.5% when cut on a data point at the top of the arch; 7% when a closed spline is cut open), and the hint says so. Control-point splines and ellipses are not split. Undo takes the split back in one step.
- **A Project curve command.** Features > Reference > **Project curve** projects a sketch, or an earlier Edit / Project curve selected in the tree, onto the faces you picked (none picked: every face of the part) as one smooth curve lying on the surface, for a groove path or a guide curve. The editor has the source, the faces (Use selection), the direction (both ways, along or against the sketch normal), the degree (2 to 9, flagged before the kernel is asked) and the fit tolerance. The sketch's own Project tool is a different thing and is unchanged.
- **Edit curve and Freeform commands.** **Edit curve** (Features > Reference) rebuilds the picked edges, the curves of a sketch or an earlier curve as one curve: by number of control points or by tolerance, with keep ends, end tangents and corners, flattening onto a plane, and a list of control point edits (point number, offset along the world axes or along the curve, weight, skip). It starts by tolerance with corners kept, so a sketch with a corner is not refused. **Freeform** (Surfaces > Edit) rebuilds one picked face as a net of control points (degree and spans, 5 x 5 points by default) and lists point moves (points as column,row, offset along the world axes, the face or a plane, spread to neighbours, mirror about a plane). Not done: dragging control points in the viewport (the offsets are typed), adding edges to an Edit curve after it is made, and the net's `of` mapping.
- **Feature commands doc and creator header no longer say Scale and Move face have no command.** Scale has its own command (Features > Modify) and Move face / Move body come from the positioning ball; the stale note that the kernel answers `TODO` for them is gone, and the list of features still without a command is corrected.
- **Export 3MF.** **Export, 3MF (3D printing)** writes a `.3mf` file: a zip with the model XML, units in millimetres, Z up as in the app, one object per body named as in the Bodies list, and the body's colour and opacity when it has one. It uses the same triangles and the same watertight check as STL and OBJ, and respects **Only visible bodies** and the mesh quality and chord boxes. Written by hand, with no new dependency.
- **Export glTF (GLB).** **Export, glTF (GLB)** writes a single binary `.glb` (glTF 2.0) with positions, normals and indices, and one node and material per body (colour, opacity, and metallic and roughness when set). glTF is Y up and in metres, so the part is turned (the model's Z axis becomes the file's Y axis) and scaled by 0.001; a 10 mm cube is 0.01 m. Tested by reading the files back: counts, bounds and volume of a cube and a cylinder, and the GLB header and chunks.
- **The Fillet editor now offers Full round.** Tick Full round and pick the two side faces and the centre face between them; the rolling ball removes the centre face (a 10 mm high box with the sides 20 mm apart loses its flat top to two 10 mm rounds). The radius, profile and edge list are hidden while it is on, since a full round reads none of them.
- **The Extrude editor now has a Twist.** Twist over the depth (degrees, positive counter-clockwise about the sketch normal) turns each slice about an axis through the sketch point you give (the sketch origin by default); the volume stays area times depth and the shape becomes a screw. It is hidden with a draft angle or Up to next, which the kernel cannot combine with it.
- **The Thicken editor now has Variable thickness.** Tick it to give the thickness as stops (2 to 6 positions along X, Y or Z with a thickness at each) instead of one value, so a blade or fin tapers root to tip in one step; a sheet from 2 mm to 3 mm thick over 100 mm of a 100 by 50 sheet is 12500 mm3 (the mean thickness), not 10000. The stops must cover the whole surface.
- **The Loft editor now has a Centre line and Connectors.** Choose an open sketch as the Centre line and the sections follow it (a bottle neck, a duct, a handle); a straight one changes nothing. Connectors set where each closed profile starts, as a fraction of its length, so two matching rectangles can be joined a corner round.
- **A SolidWorks part opens with its feature history when it can be rebuilt.** Opening a `.SLDPRT` used to give one "Imported STEP body" feature. The app now first asks the kernel to rebuild the part's history from the file's feature tree and the feature that made each face (sketches on planes, extrudes and cuts, revolves, lofts, fillets, chamfers, holes, shells), and when that gives a part that regenerates to the volume of the file's solid, the feature tree shows those features and they can be edited like any others. A part that cannot be rebuilt to the end (a feature the reader does not know yet, or a rebuild that does not match) opens as before, as one imported body, and so does any file opened with a kernel that lacks the history message; a notice says which one you got. On the local test files, 23 of 41 part files open with a history (for example a cube with a fillet is sketch, extrude, fillet; a shelled cube is sketch, extrude, shell), the rest as one body. A history that stops part way is not used yet.
- **Adding edges to a saved Fillet or Chamfer later gives what picking them all at once gives.** Edges clicked while a Fillet or Chamfer editor was open were read on the body on screen, which already has that fillet applied. There a rounded edge has cut its neighbours short or into a long piece and a stub, and the fillet has made edges of its own, so on a hub-and-petals part, rounding a hub arc and one petal flank with Tangent chain on, then reopening the fillet to add the next flank, listed that flank twice (the stub joined the chain) and the model failed with "an edge it uses no longer exists". Now each clicked edge is taken to the edge it was on the body the feature is applied to (either piece of a cut edge is that one edge; an edge the feature made itself is not added), and the tangent chain is followed on that body. The edge list also names each edge once: two entries that name the same edge are kept as one, so the duplicate chip goes away. The edges a click names now also come from the preview on screen rather than the last saved body.
- **Several edges can be picked for a Fillet or Chamfer on a phone or tablet.** Adding an edge needed Ctrl, Cmd or Shift held while clicking, and a touch screen has none, so each tap replaced the edge. Now, as in Onshape, while the Edges field of an open editor is active (Fillet, Chamfer and every other command with an Edges field open with it active; **Use selection** turns it on, **Done** off), each click or tap adds the edge to the field and a click or tap on an edge already in it takes it out, with or without a modifier key. A tap on empty space no longer clears the field, and a box selection only adds. The field shows the edges as chips with their count ("3 edges"); the x on a chip removes that one edge, which is also how to take out an edge the preview has already rounded. **Use selection** now adds the selected edges to the field instead of replacing it. A Faces list field takes clicks the same way while it is active.
- **Ticking Tangent chain in a Fillet or Chamfer editor now adds the chain to edges picked without it.** The box only re-applied the chain to an edge list it had just made itself; for edges picked with the chain off, or a fillet made in an earlier session, ticking it changed nothing, so on a petal outline the rim fillet stopped where a straight side runs into the round petal end. Ticking it now follows the chain from the feature's edges on the body the feature is applied to (here it adds the five round ends, and the fillet runs all round the rim), and unticking it again restores the list as it was.
- **The Extrude Shapes list now ticks what is actually built, and ticking a hole fills it.** A new Extrude or Revolve with no shape choice builds by even-odd nesting: a shape inside another is its hole, a shape inside that hole is solid again. The Shapes list still showed every box ticked, and ticking every box sent "no choice", so the boxes said one thing and the model did another. For example, in a disc with a ring of six petals and a centre hole, every box ticked built only the part outside the ring and petals, plus the hole's disc (3183 mm² instead of the 4976 mm² of the disc less the hole). Now the boxes start as the default: the holes are unticked. Ticking every box lists every shape, so the holes are filled (the whole disc, 5027 mm²), and every box except the hole leaves the hole open (4976 mm²). Shapes that share curves, like a ring cut by petals or a rectangle split by a diagonal, count as one level, so a petal is never treated as a hole of its ring. A thin wall still uses every shape by default. Existing parts are unchanged.
- **PET bottle sample: a smooth petaloid base, with no sharp edges.** The base was five lens-shaped grooves cut into a revolved bottle, whose edges the kernel cannot fillet. It is now built round from the start: the revolved outline is shelled to a tube, and the base is two lofts (the outer solid and the cavity one wall inside it) through 14 round sections from the body circle down to the standing plane. Each section is a closed loop of tangent arcs, five fat convex feet joined by five small concave valley arcs, so the feet curl under like the 9 mm base corner, the valley ribs rise and run out vertical into the cylinder, and the push-up centre is a cone rounded into the feet and the crown with tangent arcs. The bottle is a valid 0.35 mm shell (about 14.6 cm3 of plastic without the thread) and the sharpest angle between neighbouring facets in the base is under 30 degrees. FEET works from 3 to 7 and WALL up to 0.5 mm; the variables GROOVE_UP and DRAFT are gone. `loft()` in a script takes `newBody: 'Name'` like `extrude()`, `revolve()` and `sweep()`, so a loft can be kept as a separate body (here the cavity, cut from the bottle afterwards).
- **Rotate entities with copies no longer pins the copies to the axes.** Rotating a shape that had a vertical or horizontal lock (or a horizontal / vertical distance) with COPIES, for example five petals at 72 degrees, copied those locks onto every copy. The preview was right, but once the sketch solved the locks pulled the copies back toward the axes: wrong angles, sizes and overlaps. A rotated copy now carries only the constraints that still hold after the turn (coincident, tangent, equal, parallel, perpendicular, radius, length and angle dimensions); horizontal, vertical and horizontal / vertical distances are copied only when the copy is a whole number of quarter turns from the original (swapped to the other axis on an odd number), and a fixed point is never copied. Move, Copy and Scale were not affected (a straight move keeps every lock), and Mirror and the Linear and Circular patterns do not copy the original's constraints.
- **Calmer constraint markers in the sketch.** The glyph chips were 22 px each, one per line, and covered a drawn rectangle. They are now 14 px (1 px border, 10 px glyph) in muted colours and semi-transparent until hovered; the hit target is 24 px on a desktop pointer and still 44 px on a phone or tablet. A new setting, **Settings, Sketching, Constraint markers**, chooses **Hovered or selected** (the default: chips for what is under the pointer or selected), **Always** or **Off**; a conflicting or redundant constraint always shows. The horizontal and vertical locks of a closed rectangle show only on the side you hover. Every constraint is still in the Report and the constraint list.
- **Sketch dimensions are value chips and constraint markers are glyph chips.** A dimension value is drawn as a small dark rounded chip with the value in a monospace face (`Ø80.00 mm`, `40.00 mm`, `90.0°`), with pale-violet dimension and extension lines and arrowheads; reference dimensions stay grey, dashed and in brackets, and a conflicting one stays red. Constraint markers (the old `H` and `V` letters, tangent, equal, parallel, perpendicular, concentric, fix, midpoint, symmetric, coincident) are small rounded squares with an icon: hover names the constraint, a click selects it and Delete removes it. On a phone or tablet every chip has a 44 px tap target, and a glyph never sits on a dimension value.
- **Drawing a rectangle (or a circle, line or slot) is no longer pulled to the grid everywhere.** The grid snap caught the pointer within the 12 px pick radius, while the grid lines are 8 to 80 px apart (about 20 px by default), so the radius reached past the middle between two grid points and every position snapped: the W and H boxes could only show whole steps (7 and 30) and it felt as if you had to type a size. The grid now catches only within about 4 px of a grid point (a third of the pick radius, and at most a quarter of the step), so between grid points the size follows the pointer smoothly and near a grid point it still clicks onto it. Hold **Alt** or **Ctrl** to switch every snap off for one move or click (Alt on the Rectangle tool also still makes a square); **Settings > Sketch > Snap to grid**, the status bar's Grid box and the Spacing box are unchanged. The W and H boxes show the live size while you drag; typing in one fixes only that one (Tab moves to the other, Enter applies, Escape drops the typed value). Tested: the grid caught 100% of positions before and under 20% now.
- **Loops that share a corner point can be extruded one at a time.** A sketch whose closed loops meet at a shared point (five petals that all start at the sketch origin, a rectangle split by a diagonal) listed no shapes at all, so the Shapes list was empty and clicking a region did nothing: only separate loops were recognised. Each loop between the shared curves is now its own shape, named by a curve that belongs to it alone, so switching one petal on or off never switches its neighbour. Clicking inside the round end of a petal now picks it too (arcs were measured by their end points, which cut off the bulge). Separate loops, nested loops and holes are listed as before; the kernel already extruded these regions correctly.
- **Deleting a sketch no longer leaves its drawing behind.** Deleting the sketch you had open (tree trash icon or right-click Delete) removed it from the tree, but its points, lines, dimension chips and constraint chips stayed on the 3D view, and a new sketch started on top of that ghost; Undo then undid a sketch step instead of the delete. The sketch session now ends when its feature is gone (delete, undo, redo, or another part loaded): the draft is dropped, the overlay, chips, readout, tool and entity selection are cleared, and a sketch of a part you switch away from no longer shows on the next tab. A new sketch always starts from its own empty draft. A sketch that other features use is still refused (and stays open); deleting it together with them closes it. An open feature editor closes the same way.
- **Exporting the same part again does not cut the cosmetic knurls again.** The cut that puts a cosmetic knurl into a STEP, STL or OBJ file (seconds for an ordinary V knurl, up to about 40 s for a heavy diamond or pyramid) is now kept after it succeeds, and the next export of the same part with the same knurls reuses it: measured on a V pyramid of 90 grooves, the first STEP took 41 s and the second 3 ms, with the identical file. Any change to the part, the knurl, the part's name or the kernel build cuts again; a cut that failed, was cancelled or timed out is never kept. The last few results stay in memory and are also stored on this device (at most 8 results and about 96 MB, and emptied when the kernel build changes); where the browser refuses storage the app simply keeps them for the session. The notice then says the earlier cut was reused, and the "can take a minute" hint is skipped. Folder exports still cut each time.
- **PET bottle sample: a threaded neck and a screw cap that really screws on.** The bottle's neck now carries a real helical thread (28 mm finish: 25.5 mm root, 27.6 mm crest, 2.7 mm pitch, two turns; a trapezoid ridge swept along a helix and united with the shelled neck) and the cap has the matching internal thread, shaped from the gap between the neck ridges less a 0.15 mm flank clearance (0.2 mm radial), with 30 shallow grip ribs cut down its side. The two stay separate bodies with no contact (the closest faces are 0.135 mm apart); the neck ridge adds about 200 mm3 and the cap thread about 350 mm3 (less the ribs). Variables `THREAD` (0 gives the old plain neck and cap), `PITCH`, `TURNS`, `CLR` and `GRIP` (0 = smooth cap); it builds in about 8 s and exports to STEP as two solids. The thread ends are flat cuts, not run-outs, and the helix is baked from the variables when the script runs.
- **Exports include cosmetic knurls as real cuts.** A cosmetic (display only) knurl was missing from STEP, STL and OBJ files: the file held the plain cylinder, and a warning offered to convert the knurls in the part, after which you had to export again. Now Export is one step: the app copies the part on the side, cuts the knurls into the copy exactly as the Knurl wizard does with Cosmetic off, and writes the copy. The open part stays as it is (the knurl is still cosmetic, nothing is added to the history or the undo list), and the file name, units and options are the same. The notice says "Included N cosmetic knurl(s) as real cuts". A folder export (STEP, STL, frozen part file) does the same for the knurls whose cylinder is on a body of that folder. **Settings > General > Export > Include cosmetic knurls in exports** has **On** (the default), **Ask each time** (a small dialog with Include, Export without and Cancel, and a tick to not ask again) and **Off** (the plain solid and the warning with its Convert button, as before). It is kept on this device. A diamond or pyramid with round grooves, or a dense one, can take a minute or more (measured: a V pyramid of 90 grooves 39 s, a V diamond of 120 + 120 grooves more than 120 s, round diamonds 16 to 24 s before the kernel gives up), so a hint says so before it starts, the busy overlay says "Cutting knurl grooves for the export..." and has Cancel, and a hint tells you before a heavy one starts; after 120 seconds, or if the kernel cannot cut it, the export does not quietly write the plain solid: it says why and offers **Export without the knurl** or **Cancel**. With some bodies hidden, STL and OBJ keep the plain solid and the warning (the visible bodies cannot be told apart in the copy), and an STL or OBJ with a chosen mesh tolerance meshes the knurled copy at the standard tolerance and says so. DXF three-view drawings still show the plain cylinder.
- **A cosmetic knurl is no longer lost when a variable of a script-built part is changed.** Changing a variable on the Variables tab rebuilds a part made by a script, unless the model was changed by hand since. A cosmetic (display only) knurl is not a feature, so it did not count as a change and the rebuild dropped it without a word. The part now keeps its knurl, the script is not rerun, and a notice says to convert the knurl to real cuts or delete it first. Its row in the feature tree now reads "Knurl 1 (display only)", so you can tell it from a cut knurl, and the guide says that Save keeps it but other programs and exports do not see it.
- **The view cube's arrow ring is smaller on a phone, and you choose which buttons it has.** From the first real phone: the ring covered too much of the drawing. The ring is now four groups you switch on or off in its menu (the three dots): **Turn left/right**, **Turn up/down**, **Roll** and **Home**, kept on this device; a group that is off takes no room, and with all four off only the cube and the small menu button remain. A phone starts with Turn left/right alone (one arrow either side of the cube); a tablet and the desktop start with all four, as before. On a phone the arrows are drawn 32 px but keep a 44 px touch target, the cube is 96 px, and the ring is 160 x 128 px by default (it was 160 x 160) with the Fit and projection buttons in a row under it. **Show view arrows** is replaced by the four checkboxes; an earlier choice of hiding the arrows is kept as all four off.
- **A value typed into an on-canvas size label now applies on a phone.** The label's box committed only on an Enter key, and a phone keyboard's Done key sends none (closing the keyboard counts as leaving the box), so the typed number was dropped and nothing changed. Leaving the box with a number in it now commits it; Escape still cancels.
- **The size labels of a revolve resize it, and a label edit that cannot be applied says why.** Typing a number into the on-canvas label of a revolved part (a cylinder with domed ends, for example) did nothing, on a phone and on the desktop, and said nothing: the box treated the outline as something to scale about its first corner, which would have moved the pinned origin on the axis, so the edit was dropped. The labels now read what they measure: **R70** is the radius (the outline's distance from the axis) and the other number is the length along the axis including the rounded ends (150 for a cylinder 80 long with domes of radius 35, which is why it is larger than the cylinder face). Typing a radius moves the outline toward or away from the axis, which stays put, and the round ends follow so they stay tangent; typing a length moves the far end with its round end; dimensions in the sketch follow. The handle on the axis side is gone. When an edit cannot be applied (a size below 0.01, a pinned sketch point that would have to move, a revolve whose axis is slanted or does not touch the outline, or one with a full circle in it) a notice now says why and what to do, and the label keeps its old number. The same labels on a cylinder face, an end face and the revolve's row behave alike. Tested against the real kernel: radius 70 to 60 and length 150 to 170 on a dome-ended cylinder give the expected volumes.
- **The check key of a phone keyboard applies a typed size or sketch value whichever event it sends.** The on-canvas size label commits on Enter, a keyboard Done key arriving as key code 13 with another key name, a line-break input event, a change event or a blur (once, whichever comes first); Escape (also as key code 27) cancels. The sketch value fields and the typed-position fields take key code 13 and 27 the same way.

### Added
- **Pick the sketch plane in the 3D view.** Press `S` with nothing selected (or choose **Pick plane** in the sketch button, or the command **New sketch: pick a plane in the view**) and Front, Top and Right appear as translucent sheets at the origin with the axes; the sheet under the pointer turns orange and shows its name, and a click starts the sketch on it. A flat face or a datum plane you made can be clicked instead. On touch, a tap near a sheet's outline counts, so an edge-on sheet is easy to hit. `Esc` or Cancel gives up. The tree and the existing commands work as before.
- **Sketch Report.** A new **Sketch Report** button in the Status group of the sketch ribbon (also in the command search) opens a summary of the sketch: its name and state (fully defined, degrees of freedom left, over-constrained, conflicting constraints, does not solve), counts of points, lines, arcs, circles, splines and construction entities, closed profiles and open chains, constraints by kind, driving and reference dimensions, and the list of conflicting and redundant constraints; clicking one selects the geometry it names. A small window on a desktop, a bottom sheet on a phone or tablet; it follows your edits while open.
- **The pointer's sketch coordinates beside the pointer.** While a sketch is open, a small label follows the mouse a few pixels down and right of it and says the position on the sketch plane in the display unit (`5.61 mm, -7.13 mm`), at the snapped point when a snap is active. It is hidden for touch, in the phone layout and when the pointer leaves the view; the status bar position stays. Switch it off with **Sketching** in the settings (on by default).
- **Preview: an Icon set setting with SolidCraft's icons.** Settings, General has a new **Icon set** choice: Default (our own icons) or SolidCraft (preview). SolidCraft's two-tone icons replace ours for about 160 commands (the sketch tools and constraints, the feature tools, files, edit, display and evaluate); every command without a match keeps our icon. The choice applies at once, is remembered on the device, and follows the light and dark themes (sketch tools are green, constraints red, construction orange, surface tools orange, the rest blue, as in SolidCraft). The icons are used under the MIT licence with their attribution (see THIRD_PARTY.md); this is an experiment to judge the look, and Default stays the default.
- **View cube with an arrow ring: flip the view from a finger.** The cube is now a chamfered cube whose six faces, twelve edge bevels and eight corners are all targets (the one under the pointer or finger lights up), with readable FRONT, BACK, LEFT, RIGHT, TOP and BOTTOM labels. Around it: four arrows that turn the view a step (tap, or hold to keep turning), two curved arrows that roll the view clockwise and counter-clockwise, **Home** (isometric view) and a menu to set the step (15, 30, 45 or 90 degrees) or hide the arrows. The buttons are 44 px on a phone and tablet; the ring is open by default on a phone and sits in the top right corner clear of the middle of the drawing. Views from the cube, the arrows and Home are animated (reduced motion: instant). Four 90 degree steps return exactly; from an iso view left and right keep the tilt and up and down stop on Top or Bottom.
- **Export a feature-tree folder as a part.** Right-click a folder: **Export folder geometry** as STEP, STL or a frozen `.ckpart` writes the bodies the folder's features created or changed (picked from the face names of the built model, then cut out of a scratch copy of the document, so the open model is untouched and the file holds the kernel's exact solids). A body another folder's features also changed comes out whole, with a notice. **Extract folder as a part** opens the folder's features, with the variables they use, as a new document; it is refused naming the features, faces or bodies from outside the folder that it uses, and when the extract does not build to the volume of the folder's bodies. Refused with a hint: a rolled-back model, a folder with only sketches or planes, one whose solid features are all suppressed, one that left no body. STEP re-imports to the same volume (910 mm3 for the test pad), the STL is watertight, the frozen part reopens with the same volume. Not built: offering to include the outside features of an extract, a body's colours in the exported part file, assembly instances. `apps/cad/src/folderExport.ts`.
- **Phone: one slim top bar, tools above the bottom bar, properties that share the screen, number keypads and drawing by touch.** Fixes from the first real phone. The top two rows (file buttons, mode tabs, search, document strip, ribbon tabs and tools) are gone: the top bar is the file menu, the document title (tap for the open documents, New part), undo, redo and a more menu; the ribbon is **Tools** in the bottom bar, which opens one labelled strip of the chosen tab's tools just above it and closes when a tool is picked or the drawing tapped. With every sheet closed the two bars take about 100 px and the drawing 88% of the screen height; the tablet gets one top bar row and no strip for one document. **Properties no longer cover the drawing**: the tree, properties and diagnostics sheets take their own row, the canvas shrinks to the room above (keeping its framing, so the model stays in view and updates while a value is typed), an editor sheet never rises above 60% of the screen (the tree and diagnostics lists may), and with the on-screen keyboard up the bars give way and the sheet and the focused field stay visible. **Numeric keyboard**: number fields ask for the decimal keypad (counts: digits) with a Done or Next key, formula fields get an ƒ key (full keyboard) and a ± key, and the on-canvas value label opens its box with the digit keypad. **Sketching by touch**: with a sketch tool active one finger no longer orbits; press sets the first point (a circle's centre), drag sizes the shape with a live preview and lift places it, two fingers pan and pinch, tap-tap still works, a second finger or a cancelled touch drops the shape in progress, and a circle dragged out this way is selected with its radius field focused. Pick and snap tolerance is 24 px for a finger. ## 0.3.9 - 2026-10-27

## 0.3.8 - 2026-10-27

### Added
- **Scale command.** Features > Modify > Scale scales the whole part about its centre of mass (a factor of 2 to start). The options panel takes one factor, or one per axis, the point it grows from (centre of mass, the origin, or a point) and shows the bodies it acts on. Per-axis stretches stay exact for planes, cylinders and cones along their axis and B-splines; the kernel refuses (and says why) what it cannot keep exact, for example a cylinder stretched across its axis.

### Fixed

## 0.3.7 - 2026-10-26

### Fixed
- **Big multi-body files rebuild in seconds, not minutes (kernel).** Every boolean result re-checks the volume of each face, and the table that remembers them held 8,192 faces and emptied itself when full; SELENE-functional has 12,000, so it never hit and recomputed all of them for every cut. The table now holds a quarter of a million. The whole SELENE-functional file now rebuilds in about 40 s; before, it did not finish in ten minutes.
- **SolidCraft import: features of a component that act on a transferred body now run.** A body handed to a component by a body transfer is still built where it was made, so the component's moves, scales, cuts and removes of it found "no such body" and were dropped. With both components at the identity they now run in the part that holds the body. SELENE-functional goes from about 150 unsupported rows to the 15 that need the kernel to find edges (fillets and shells), its 8 scales convert, and its Pi board gets its holes.
- **A cut that names `participants`** (not only `targets`) cuts those bodies, as SolidCraft's own evaluator does; before, such a cut was applied to everything.

## 0.3.6 - 2026-10-26

### Added
- **Compressed parts.** Save As has a "Compress" box, which saves the part as `name.ckpart.gz` (gzip). A part is very repetitive JSON: SELENE's 1.9 MB comes out at 138 KB. Opening, dropping and the recent list accept `.ckpart.gz`, and any file that starts with the gzip header is read as compressed whatever its name. Saving over an open `.gz` file keeps it compressed. Plain `.ckpart` stays the default and stays plain JSON, so other tools and diffs still work.

## 0.3.5 - 2026-10-26

### Added
- **Scale.** The kernel scales bodies about a point by one factor or one per axis (`scale`), exactly where the shape stays exact: planes, cylinders and cones stretched along their axis, free-form faces, and spheres and tori under one factor. SELENE's own 8 scales are inside components that get their bodies by a body transfer, which is not supported yet, so they are still reported as unsupported there.
- **Extrema query (kernel, for agents and tools):** the extent of a body or selection along a direction, with the extreme points and what they lie on.

## 0.1.0 - 2026-10-25

First versioned release; this entry covers the last day of work.

- **Sphere feature.** A kernel `sphere` (centre and radius, one spherical face; add, cut or new body) with its editor and tree icon. The Catalog's solid sphere is now one row.
